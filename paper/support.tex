\section{Every model compared}
\label{sec:support}

\label{sec:sup-comparison}
Every analysis in this and the next five sections (the ablation, the occlusion,
the carried-state tests, the calibration and reliability of the forecast ranges,
and the decision costs) was rerun on the tuned models of
Table~\ref{tab:final-settings}, on the same rolling windows as
Section~\ref{sec:results}; none of its numbers comes from the settings of the
first version.


\textbf{The tuned pair and their average are more accurate than every model not
tuned on the data, on every data set but one.} Table~\ref{tab:sup-comparison}
adds the pretrained models, the Temporal Fusion Transformer and seasonal naive.
TimesFM given the plan is the most accurate of the models not trained on the
data on \supTfmPlanBestUntrained{} data sets. On Frat it is more accurate than
either tuned model alone (\supFratTfmPlan{} against \supFratWm{} for the world
model and \supFratLgb{} for LightGBM) but not than their average
(\supFratAvg{}). Timestral given the plan forecasts far above sales on the
weekly data, where a few extreme upper quantiles dominate the mean of its
output: \supAnaTstPlan{} on analgesics against \supAnaTfmPlan{} for TimesFM given
the plan.

\section{Ablation: what each model adds to the combination}
\label{sec:sup-ablation}

\textbf{Each tuned model makes the combination more accurate, and TimesFM adds a
little at item by store.} Table~\ref{tab:sup-ablation} drops each member of the
three-model ensemble in turn. Without the world model the ensemble is less
accurate item by store on \supNoWmWorse{} of \supAblationSets{} data sets, and
clearly less accurate in \supPNoWmWorse{} of \supPNoWmN{} window comparisons
(clearly more accurate in \supPNoWmBetter{}); without LightGBM, in
\supPNoLgbWorse{} (clearly more accurate in \supPNoLgbBetter{}). Without TimesFM,
which leaves the average of the two tuned models, it is clearly less accurate item
by store in \supPNoTfmWorse{} comparisons and clearly more accurate in
\supPNoTfmBetter{}. Item by store, then, adding TimesFM with the plan to the
average helps; over all levels the average is in the model confidence set more
often (Section~\ref{sec:rq-grain}). A retailer whose main use is store orders can
add it at the cost of a third model to run. Weights fitted on earlier windows to
the item-by-store error beat equal weights in \supPFitBottomBetter{} of
\supPFitBottomN{} comparisons and lost in \supPFitBottomWorse{}; weights fitted to
the error over all levels beat them in \supPFitAllBetter{} and lost in
\supPFitAllWorse{}.

Figure~\ref{fig:sup-ablation}a shows the same comparison data set by data set.
Dropping the world model raises the item-by-store error on \sfAblNoWmWorse{}
data sets, dropping LightGBM on \sfAblNoLgbWorse{} and dropping TimesFM on
\sfAblNoTfmWorse{}; fitted weights lower the error most on frozen entrees,
bottled juice and snacks. Fitted to the item-by-store error, the mean
weights are \sfWtWmBottomMean{} for the world model, \sfWtLgbBottomMean{} for
LightGBM and \sfWtTfmBottomMean{} for TimesFM given the plan
(Figure~\ref{fig:sup-ablation}b); fitted to the total, TimesFM's weight falls to
\sfWtTfmTotalMean{}, and the weights move from data set to data set.

\section{Occlusion: which plan inputs each model uses}
\label{sec:sup-plan}

\textbf{Both tuned models forecast from the plan.} Setting the price to the
regular price makes the item-by-store error of the world model worse on
\supPertPriceHelpsWm{} of \supPertSets{} data sets and of LightGBM on
\supPertPriceHelpsLgb{} (Favorita carries no price, so its forecasts do not
change); setting every plan input to nothing planned makes it
worse on \supPertPlanHelpsWm{} and \supPertPlanHelpsLgb{}
(Table~\ref{tab:sup-perturbation}). With the plan removed, the world model's
forecast on the rows where the plan had something on falls by
\supPertUnitsWmLo\% to \supPertUnitsWmHi\%, and LightGBM's by
\supPertUnitsLgbLo\% to \supPertUnitsLgbHi\%: the two read about the same
lift from a promotion. Cutting the world model's history to the last few periods
makes its error worse on \supPertHistHelps{} of \supPertSets{}, so its carried
state draws on more than the latest sales.

Figure~\ref{fig:sup-occlusion} breaks the plan into its parts. On the
Dominick's categories and Frat, price and the promotion flag carry the plan;
display, feature advertising and a price cut on its own add less on Frat, the one
data set that records them. The promotion flag helps the world model on
\sfPromoWmHelps{} of the \sfPromoSets{} data sets that carry it and LightGBM on
\sfPromoLgbHelps{}. Events, SNAP days and paydays change the error by at most
\sfCalendarMax{}. Shuffling an input across the periods of each series, which
keeps how often it is on but breaks its timing, moves the error the same way as
switching it off for \sfPermAgree{} of \sfPermPairs{} pairs of input and data
set. Keeping only the last few periods of history raises the world model's error
on \sfHistShortWorse{} of \sfHistSets{} data sets at the shortest cut
(Figure~\ref{fig:sup-occlusion}c). The cut lengths are 4, 13 and 26 periods on
every data set but M5, which used 28 and 56 days; on Favorita, which is daily,
the shortest keeps four days and the error rises by \sfFavHistFour{}.

\section{Carried-state tests: what the world model's state does}
\label{sec:sup-mechanism}

\textbf{The tuned world model uses the timing of the plan, and carries a
promotion forward as more sales rather than a dip.} Shuffling the plan's timing
within each series makes its item-by-store error worse on all \supMechSets{}
data sets, by \supTimingLo\% to \supTimingHi\% (Table~\ref{tab:sup-mechanism}):
the forecast reads when a promotion runs, not only that one runs. A promotion and
a price cut added in one period raise that period's forecast and still raise the
next on \supPersistRiseSets{} of \supMechSets{} data sets (the change in the next
period runs from \supPersistTwoLo\% to +\supPersistTwoHi\% of the forecast
without them), where shoppers who stocked up would buy less; the dip
after a promotion is not in the forecast, as in the first version. The state
rolled through the window predicts sales after it only a little better than the
state before it (an $R^2$ gain of \supProbeGainMin{} to \supProbeGainMax{}).

\section{Calibration and reliability of the forecast ranges}
\label{sec:sup-calibration}

\textbf{The ranges are calibrated item by store and too narrow above the
store.}
Each model's ranges come from its own errors in the earlier windows
(Section~\ref{sec:setup}). Item by store, the 80\% ranges of all three forecasts
cover close to 80\% of sales across data sets and windows (\supCovIxsWm\%,
\supCovIxsLgb\% and \supCovIxsAvg\% for the world model, LightGBM and the
average), and the average has the lowest scaled pinball loss (\supSplIxsAvg{},
against \supSplIxsWm{} and \supSplIxsLgb{}). At the chain total, where each window
adds one error to learn from, the world model's ranges are too narrow
(\supCovTotalWm\% for 80\%) and the average's are close to target
(\supCovTotalAvg\%); the coverage of a single data set swings with so few earlier
windows (Table~\ref{tab:sup-intervals}).

Figure~\ref{fig:sup-calibration} sets each stated coverage against the share of
sales that fell inside. Item by store, the 50\% ranges hold \sfCovFiftyIxsWm\%,
\sfCovFiftyIxsLgb\% and \sfCovFiftyIxsAvg\% of sales for the world model,
LightGBM and the average, and the 95\% ranges \supCovNinetyFiveIxsWm\%,
\supCovNinetyFiveIxsLgb\% and \supCovNinetyFiveIxsAvg\%, so all three lie on the
diagonal on average. Data set by data set the 80\% coverage item by store runs
from \sfCovEightyMinIxsAvg\% to \sfCovEightyMaxIxsAvg\% for the average. At the
total the world model's 80\% coverage runs from \sfCovEightyMinTotalWm\% to
\sfCovEightyMaxTotalWm\% across data sets; a single window at the total holds
one actual against one range, so its coverage is anywhere from
\sfCovTotalWindowMin\% to \sfCovTotalWindowMax\%, and the
\sfCalWindows{} to \sfCalWindowsMax{} scored windows of each data set give a coarse
estimate. By level (Figure~\ref{fig:sup-calibration}c), the 80\% ranges are too
narrow at the store (\sfCovLevelStoreWm\%, \sfCovLevelStoreLgb\% and
\sfCovLevelStoreAvg\%) and for the item across stores (\sfCovLevelItemWm\%,
\sfCovLevelItemLgb\% and \sfCovLevelItemAvg\%): the earlier windows' errors at
those levels understate the next window's. A planner who orders from the 80\%
range at item by store gets what it says; one who plans store labour from the
store-level range needs a wider one.

\paragraph{The whole distribution} Coverage checks three ranges;
Figure~\ref{fig:sup-probabilistic} checks every quantile of the forecast
distribution and scores it whole (Table~\ref{tab:sup-probabilistic}). Item by
store, \sfRelTenAvg\% of actuals fall below the average's 10th percentile,
\sfRelHalfAvg\% below its median and \sfRelNinetyAvg\% below its 90th: the
lower quantiles of a slow item are zero and many actuals are zero too, so the
curve is read at the midpoint of the shares strictly below and at or below each
quantile. At the total, \sfRelTenTotWm\% of actuals fall below the world model's
10th percentile and \sfRelNinetyTotWm\% below its 90th; for LightGBM the shares
are \sfRelTenTotLgb\% and \sfRelNinetyTotLgb\%. %PROBTOTAL%
On the continuous ranked probability score (CRPS), which scores the whole
distribution, the average is lowest item by store on \sfCrpsAvgBestIxs{} of
\sfProbSets{} data sets, LightGBM on \sfCrpsLgbBestIxs{} and the world model on
\sfCrpsWmBestIxs{}; at the total the average is lowest on \sfCrpsAvgBestTot{},
LightGBM on \sfCrpsLgbBestTot{} and the world model on \sfCrpsWmBestTot{}.

\section{Decision costs and reconciliation}
\label{sec:sup-decisions}

\paragraph{Decision costs}
The average gives the lowest category-plan cost on \supCatPlanAvgLowest{} of
\supCatPlanSets{} data sets and LightGBM on the other \supCatPlanLgbLowest{}
(frozen entrees and snacks); the world model alone is lowest on none
(Table~\ref{tab:sup-decisions}). Ordering each forecast's 0.8 quantile for every
item and store costs less with the average than with LightGBM on
\supOrderAvgBelowLgb{} data sets and less than with the world model on
\supOrderAvgBelowWm{}. Both are simulations under stated costs, not business
results.

\paragraph{Reconciliation}
Summing the average upward is more accurate over all levels than the best
reconciled ETS forecast on \supAvgBeatsEts{} of \supReconSets{} data sets
(Table~\ref{tab:sup-reconcile}). The exceptions are soft drinks and snacks,
where reconciled ETS is more accurate at the coarse levels, which carry most of
the all-level score; combining either tuned model with ETS above it does not do
better than the average on most data sets.
